\begin{abstract}
\addchaptertocentry{\abstractname}
This thesis investigates \ac{qmc} methods from their theoretical foundations to applications in machine learning and medical imaging. \ac{qmc} methods replace random sampling by low-discrepancy sequences, thereby improving uniformity and under suitable smoothness conditions convergence rates compared to classical \ac{mc} sampling.

Part~\ref{part1} develops the mathematical framework of \ac{qmc} integration, introducing concepts such as discrepancy, Hardy--Krause variation and the Koksma--Hlawka inequality, which provides a deterministic error bound and serves as the theoretical basis for the subsequent studies.

Part~\ref{part2} examines the use of \ac{qmc} sampling in the training of \ac{dnn}. By revisiting and refining recent theoretical results, it is shown that applying the Koksma--Hlawka inequality to the generalization gap requires stricter regularity assumptions than previously stated. Empirical experiments on benchmark problems confirm that \ac{qmc}-based training sets can reduce generalization error and thus constitute a promising alternative to random sampling in many-query problems.

Part~\ref{part3} introduces a \ac{qmc}-based framework for photon transport simulation in X-ray imaging. By integrating \ac{qmc} sampling into photon generation and propagation, the framework achieves measurable improvements in accuracy and noise reduction compared to standard \ac{mc} simulations across different phantom geometries. These results highlight the potential of \ac{qmc} methods for scatter estimation in computed tomography.

Overall, the thesis demonstrates both the mathematical foundations and the practical versatility of \ac{qmc} methods, establishing them as valuable tools across distinct domains of computational mathematics.
\end{abstract}
