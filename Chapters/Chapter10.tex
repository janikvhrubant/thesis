\chapter{The Simulation Algorithm}
\label{chapter10}

In this chapter, the algorithms developed for X-ray image simulation are
presented. To enhance convergence, they integrate different sampling techniques
and aspects of the \emph{Forced Fixed Detection} approach.

In standard \ac{mc} simulations a random variable determines whether a photon
escapes the phantom or undergoes a scattering event. Thus, only a fraction of
the simulated photons reaches the detector and contributes to the final image.
To attain a satisfactory photon distribution at the detector, a very large
number of photons with differrent directions, energies and scatter behaviors
must be simulated. To reduce the variance and accelerate convergence,
low-discrepancy sequences are used in this thesis to sample the initial photon
properties and the stochastic processes during photon transport.

For comparing the performance of the conservative approach of \ac{mc} sampling the model, to the proposed Sobol' and Halton based sampling, a simulation algorithm has been developed, which is based on the \emph{Forced Fixed Detection} approach. Forced fixed detection is a further variance reduction technique, which is applied in \cite{fd2001,qmcXray2023}. Therefore, a fixed number of photon scatter events is predefined. Then, at each scatter event, the escape probability is calculated. This represents the intensity of the photon energy which is not being scattered but instead directly forced to contribute to the detector signal. For the remaining intensity, a free path length is sampled from the exponential distribution according to Beer-Lambert's law and the photon is scattered. Again, the photon intensity is updated according to the probability of staying in the phantom and being scattered respectively. Thus every photon that is simulated definetly contributes to the detector signal, which is not the case in conventional \ac{mc} simulations.

\section{Preliminaries}

Before delving into the detailed description of the simulation algorithm, some
preliminary assumptions are outlined. 

First, the the phantom is assumed to be surrounded by air. Furthermore, the
attenuation of air is neglected. Thus, it is assumed that no attenuation occurs outside the phantom.

The phantom is assumed to be convex, such that a photon, which left the phantom,
cannot re-enter it. This simpliefies the implementation, however, the algorithm
can be extended to handle non-convex phantoms as well. Either by applying the
ray traversal algorithm multiple times to check whether the photon re-enters the
phantom or by setting the attenuation of air to a very small value.

The positions of the photons are represented in spherical coordinates with a
specific unit scale. The \emph{voxel size} $\lambda$ represents the size of a
voxel in centimeters and is used for scaling. Thus, a photon with position
$P=(x,y,z)$ denoted in centimeters, corresponds to the voxel coordinates
$P'=(\frac{x}{\lambda}, \frac{y}{\lambda}, \frac{z}{\lambda})$.

\section{Algorithm overview}
\label{sec:algorithmOverview}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The algorithm begins by initializing with a sampling technique (e.g. Monte
Carlo, Sobol', Halton), a predefined number of photons, the beam geometry, the
phantom geometry and a fixed number of scatter events $N$.

Then a total of $3(N+1)$ random values $(u_k)_{k=0}^{3(N+1)}$ per photon are
sampled according to the sampling technique chosen.

With that in place, a set of photons, each assigned an initial energy $E_0$
sampled from the X-ray tube spectrum, an initial position $S$ and an initial
direction $\vec{\omega}_0$ sampled according to the beam geometry is chosen as
described in Section~\ref{sec:photonDirectionSampling}. To sample the initial
position, direction and energy of each photon, a total of three random values is
required per photon: $u_0, u_1, u_2$.

The photons are placed within a voxel grid composed of cubic voxels that define
the geometry of the scene. Each voxel encodes an integer value identifying a
specific material or tissue type and density.

Furthermore, each photon is initialized with an intensity of $W_0=1$, which is
iteratively updated throughout the simulation to account for attenuation and
scattering processes.

Next a ray traversal algorithm (Section~\ref{sec:rayTraversal}) is applied to
traverse the photon through air until it reaches the phantom. Hereby, the
traversed voxels and according distances $d_j$ are captured. Beginning at the
entry point of the phantom $A_0$, attenuation effects are happening and the
adapted \emph{Forced Fixed Detection algorithm} is applied.

Therefore, at every point $A_i$ with $i \in \{0, \ldots, N-1\}$, the escape probability

\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:escapeProbability}
    p(A_i, \vec{\omega}_i, E_i) := \exp\bigg(-\int\limits_{\overrightarrow{A_iC_i}} \mu(x, E_i) dx\bigg)
\end{equation}

is computed according to Beer-Lambert's law (Eq.~\ref{eq:lambert_beer_law})
depending on the current direction $\vec{\omega}_i$ and photon energy $E_i$.
$C_i$ denotes the exit point of the phantom in photon direction. $\mu$ refers to
the attenuation coefficient depending on the position $x$ in the phantom and the
photon energy $E_i$.

Then, the free path length $t_i$ is sampled attributing the random value
$u_{3i}$ while forcing the photon to stay within the phantom. The \emph{forced}
aspect of the forced fixed detection algorithm implies that the photon is
guaranteed to scatter $N$ times within the phantom before completely exiting the
phantom. This is ensured by forcing the next scatter point to be inbetween
the current position $A_i$ and the exit point $C_i$. To apply the uniform
distribution of $u_{3i}$, a target value is computed according to Beer-Lambert's
law \eqref{eq:lambert_beer_law}:

\begin{equation*}
    \exp(-\int\limits_0^{t_i} \mu(A_{i} + s \cdot \vec{\omega}_{i}, E_{i}) ds) = 1 - u_{3i} * (1 - p(A_i, \vec{\omega}_i, E_i))\,.
\end{equation*}

As the target value needs to be in the range $[p(A_i, \vec{\omega}_i, E_i), 1]$,
the right hand side is constructed as such. Consequently, the following equation
is solved for $t_i$ as in
Algorithm~\ref{alg:forcedDetectionStep} (line~\ref{alg:forcedDetectionStep:line:freePathLength}-\ref{alg:forcedDetectionStep:line:freePathLengthEnd}):

\begin{equation}
    \int\limits_0^{t_i} \mu(A_{i} + s \cdot \vec{\omega}_{i}, E_{i}) ds = 
    -\ln(1-u_{3i} * (1-p(A_i, \vec{\omega}_i, E_i)))\,.
    \label{eq:freePathLength}
\end{equation}

Then, the next scatter point is computed as:

\begin{equation*}
    A_{i+1} = A_i + t_i \cdot \vec{\omega}_i
\end{equation*}

Further, the following two scenarios are considered at each forced scatter
point $i \in \{0, \ldots, N\}$:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Photon escapes the phantom without further scattering:}\\
        Proportional to the probability of escaping the phantom
        $p(A_i,\vec{\omega}_i,E_i)$, the intensity and photon energy contributes
        to the detector signal without further scattering with intensity
        \begin{equation*}
            W^{\text{escape}}_{i+1} = W_i \cdot p(A_i, \vec{\omega}_i, E_i)\,.
        \end{equation*}
        The accounting detector pixel is determined in
        Algorithm~\ref{alg:forcedDetectionStep} (line
        \ref{alg:forcedDetectionStep:line:ExitPoint}).
        In case $i=0$, this intensity is accounted as primary intensity.
    \item \textbf{Photons stays in the phantom and scatters:}\\
        The leftover intensity $W_i \cdot (1 - p(A_i, \vec{\omega}_i, E_i))$
        splits into two: the absorbed intensity and the Compton scattered
        intensity. This ratio is determined by the ratio of the Compton
        scattering coefficient $\mu_{\text{CS}}$ and the photoelectric
        coefficient $\mu_{\text{\text{PE}}}$. Thus, the leftover intensity after
        Compton scattering is
        \begin{equation*}
            W_{i+1} = W_i \cdot (1 - p(A_i, \vec{\omega}_i, E_i)) \cdot \frac{\mu_{\text{CS}}(A_{i+1}, E_i)}{\mu_{\text{CS}}(A_{i+1}, E_i) + \mu_{\text{PE}}(A_{i+1}, E_i)}\,.
        \end{equation*}
        Within Algorithm~\ref{alg:forcedDetectionStep} (line \ref{alg:forcedDetectionStep:line:compton}), the new
        photon energy $E_{i+1}$ and direction $\vec{\omega}_{i+1}$ after the
        Compton scatter event is sampled with two more random variables
        $u_{3i+1}$ and $u_{3i+2}$.
\end{itemize}

This process is repeated according to the maximum scatter order $N$. The
leftover intensity $W_N$ after the last forced scatter event is forwarded to the
detector without any further scattering.

\section{Sub-Algorithms}
\label{sec:subAlgorithms}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

To model the relevant physical processes in a structured manner, the main
simulation is decomposed into several sub-algorithms. This section describes the
purpose and implementation of each sub-algorithm in detail.

\subsection{Photon Generation}
\label{sec:photonDirectionSampling}

To initialize a photon, the three properties energy, position and direction must
be sampled. The photon energy is sampled independently from the position and
direction as the spectral distribution accounts for the whole beam angle. For
the generation, a total of three random variables $u_0,u_1,u_2 \in [0,1]$ are
applied per photon.

\subsubsection{Photon Energy Sampling}

To sample the photon energy, the inverse transform sampling method is applied as
described in Section~\ref{sec:photon_energy}. To obtain the inverse function of
the \ac{cdf}, the following algorithm is adopted:

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Inverse-Determination of the CDF}
\label{alg:photonEnergySampling}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Strictly increasing energy nodes $E[1..n]$
\Require Nonnegative fluence samples $F[1..n]$ (not all zero)
\Ensure Inverse CDF represented by nodes $E[0..n]$ and values $\mathrm{CDF}[0..n]$
\State $T \gets \sum_{i=1}^{n} F[i]$ \Comment{Normalize}
\State \textbf{for} $i=1..n$ \textbf{do} $\mathrm{PDF}[i] \gets F[i]/T$
\State $\mathrm{CDF}[1] \gets \mathrm{PDF}[1]$
\State \textbf{for} $i=2..n$ \textbf{do} $\mathrm{CDF}[i] \gets \mathrm{CDF}[i-1]+\mathrm{PDF}[i]$
\State \textbf{(Guard)} set $\mathrm{CDF}[0]\gets 0$; \quad $E[0]\gets E[1]$; \quad enforce $\mathrm{CDF}[n]=1$;
\State \Return $(E[0..n],\,\mathrm{CDF}[0..n])$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

This algorithm constructs the \ac{cdf} and returns two arrays: one with the
energy nodes and one with the corresponding \ac{cdf} values. To determine the
photon energy $\mathcal{E}$ according to random variable $u_0$, a loop is
applied to find the index $j$ such that $\mathrm{CDF}[j-1] \le u_1 <
\mathrm{CDF}[j]$. The corresponding energy is then $\mathcal{E} = E[j]$.

\subsubsection{Photon Position and Direction Sampling: Point Source Model}

For the point source model, the initial position $S$ of each photon is fixed at
the source position $\mathcal{S}^*$. The direction $\vec{\omega}$ is sampled
uniformly within a cone defined by the beam axis $\vec{\textbf{a}}$ and opening
angle $\Theta$. This requires two independent random variables $u_1, u_2 \in
[0,1]$.

The following algorithm is applying the method as described in
Section~\ref{sec:photon_position_direction} and samples the direction
$\vec{\omega}$ for a beam with angle $\Theta$ in cone axis $(0,0,1)$ For general
applicability, the algorithm is projecting the cone axis for a given direction
$\vec{\textbf{a}} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Photon Direction Sampling for a Point Source}
\label{alg:pointSourceSampling}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Unit cone axis $\hat{\mathbf{a}}$
\Require Half-opening angle $\Theta$
\Require Random samples $u_1,u_2 \sim \mathcal{U}(0,1)$
\Ensure Emission direction $\vec{\omega}$

\State $\phi \gets 2\pi u_2$
\State $\cos\theta \gets 1 - u_1\,(1-\cos\Theta)$
\State $\theta \gets \arccos(\cos\theta)$
\State $\vec{w} \gets (\sin\theta\cos\phi,\;\sin\theta\sin\phi,\;\cos\theta)$ \Comment{local sample on cap}
\State Choose $\vec{t}=(0,0,1)$ if $|a_3|<0.999$, else $\vec{t}=(1,0,0)$
\State $\hat{\mathbf{u}} \gets \dfrac{\vec{t}\times\hat{\mathbf{a}}}{\|\vec{t}\times\hat{\mathbf{a}}\|}$,\quad
      $\hat{\mathbf{v}} \gets \hat{\mathbf{a}}\times\hat{\mathbf{u}}$
\State $\vec{\omega} \gets w_x\hat{\mathbf{u}} + w_y\hat{\mathbf{v}} + w_z\hat{\mathbf{a}}$
\State \Return $(\vec{\omega})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Whereas the Algorithm yields the direction $\vec{\omega}$, the position is
simply set to be the sourse position: $S = \mathcal{S}^*$

\subsubsection{Photon Position Sampling for an Extended Source}
For the extended source model, the 4 edge points of the rectangle source within
a plane $P$ must be given: $P_1, P_2, P_3, P_4 \in \mathbb{R}^3$. The initial
position $S$ of each photon is sampled by the simple algorithm conducting the
calculation from section \ref{sec:photon_position_direction}. Again two random
values $u_1, u_2 \in [0,1]$ are required.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Photon Position Sampling for an Extended Source}
\label{alg:extendedSourceSampling}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Rectangle corner points $P_1, P_2, P_3, P_4 \in \mathbb{R}^3$
\Require Random samples $u_1,u_2 \sim \mathcal{U}(0,1)$
\Ensure Emission position $S$
\State $\vec{v}_1 \gets P_2 - P_1$
\State $\vec{v}_2 \gets P_4 - P_1$
\State $S \gets P_1 + u_1 \vec{v}_1 + u_2 \vec{v}_2$
\State \Return $(S)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

For the extended source modelling, the photon direction is set to be the
perpendicular direction of the plane $P$.


\subsection{Ray Traversal}
\label{sec:rayTraversal}

The ray traversal algorithm is used for traversing the photon through the voxel
grid until either a certain material is reached or the photon exits the grid.
The algorithm is intended to stop when a certain material is reached in the voxel grid. Firstly, if the photon starts in air the algorithm is used to project the photon until it reaches the phantom. Therefore, it consistently checks whether the next voxel is still air or not. Secondly, the algorithm is used to traverse the photon through the phantom to determine the exit point $C$ of the phantom. Here, the algorithm checks whether the next algorithm is still a material that is not air. Then again, the algorithm is applied to traverse air until it exits the voxel grid and reaches the detector plane. Here, the algorithm is applied as long as the next voxel material is air. As the phantom is assumed to be convex, the photon must exit the grid after leaving the phantom and its projection through air.

The last two applications of the ray traversal algorithm are within the
\emph{Forced Fixed Detection} algorithm to traverse the photon through the
phantom and onto the detector. Hereby, it is important to capture the traversed
voxel indices with the according distances, to proportionally consider the
attenuation effects.

For the initialization of the ray traversal algorithm, the following inputs are required:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The voxel grid $materialGrid \in \mathbb{Z}^{X \times Y \times Z}$
    representing the materials of the scene including the phantom. $X,Y,Z$ represent
    the shape of the grid.
    \item The voxel size $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^+$.
    \item The initial position of the photon $A \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
    \item The (unit) direction of the photon $\vec{\omega} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
\end{itemize}

Traversing the ray in the grid means traversing the ray through the cubic
voxels. For the determination of the attenuation along the path, the precisely
determined distances within each voxel, the voxel indices and their
corresponding materials need to be tracked. For quickly determining the sampled
free path length, the starting points in each voxel are also captured. 

This algorithm proceeds voxel by voxel. To carry out the ray traversal within a
specific voxel, the component algorithm \ref{alg:rayTraversalStep} is applied at
each voxel along the ray.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Ray Traversal Algorithm: Step}
\label{alg:rayTraversalStep}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Current position in voxel grid coordinates $\tilde{A}$
\Require Current unit direction $\vec{\omega} = (\omega_x, \omega_y, \omega_z)$
\Ensure Distance to next voxel boundary $maxDist$
\Ensure Updated position $\tilde{A}'$
\For{$i \in \{x,y,z\}$}
    \If{$\omega_i > 0$}
        \State $maxDist_i \gets \dfrac{1 - (\tilde{A}_i - \lfloor\tilde{A}_i\rfloor)}{\omega_i}$
    \ElsIf{$\omega_i < 0 \;\text{and}\; \lfloor\tilde{A}_i\rfloor \neq \tilde{A}_i$}
        \State $maxDist_i \gets - \dfrac{\lfloor\tilde{A}_i\rfloor}{\omega_i}$
    \ElsIf{$\omega_i < 0 \;\text{and}\; \lfloor\tilde{A}_i\rfloor = \tilde{A}_i$}
        \State $maxDist_i \gets -\dfrac{1}{\omega_i}$
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\State $distance \gets \min(maxDist_x, maxDist_y, maxDist_z)$
\State $\tilde{A}' \gets \tilde{A} + \vec{\omega} \cdot distance$
\State \Return $maxDist, \tilde{A}'$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:rayTraversalStep} computes for each component $x,y,z$ the
distance to the next voxel boundary in the direction of the ray. Then it
normalizes the distance by the respective component of the direction vector. The
minimum of these three distances in the direction of the ray is the distance to
the next voxel boundary. The position is then updated accordingly.

The main ray traversal algorithm (Algorithm~\ref{alg:rayTraversalMain}) applies
Algorithm~\ref{alg:rayTraversalStep} iteratively until the stopping criterion is
met. Whereas the stopping criterion in the Algorithm is solely running as long as
the current material is not air, the algorithm can be easily adapted to stop at
any other material and therefore be applied in the different scenarios described
above.

Further, the algorithm converts the position $A$ in world coordinates to voxel
coordinates $\tilde{A}$ and vice versa. It captures the crossed voxel indices,
their corresponding materials, the entry points in world coordinates and the
distances in centimeters within each voxel in the arrays $crossedVoxels$,
$crossedMaterials$, $entryPoints$ and $distances$ respectively.

The algorithm returns the following values:

\begin{itemize}
    \item The array $crossedVoxels$ contains all voxel indices of voxels that the ray traversed.
    \item The array $crossedMaterials$ contains the corresponding materials of the crossed voxels.
    \item The array $entryPoints$ contains the starting points in the according voxel in world coordinates.
    \item The array $distances$ contains the distances in centimeters such that their unit is consistent with the attenuation coefficients.
    \item The exit point $C$ in world coordinates where the ray exits the phantom or the voxel grid.
\end{itemize}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
    \caption{Ray Traversal: Main Algorithm}
    \label{alg:rayTraversalMain}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require Material grid $materialGrid \in \mathbb{Z}^{X \times Y \times
        Z}$, voxel size $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^+$
        \Require Initial position $A \in \mathbb{R}^3$, unit direction $\vec{\omega} \in
        \mathbb{R}^3$
        \Ensure Final position $C \in \mathbb{R}^3$
        \Ensure Arrays $crossedVoxels, crossedMaterials, entryPoints, distances$
        \Ensure Boolean $exitGrid$ indicating whether the photon exited the grid

        \State Initialize empty arrays $crossedVoxels, crossedMaterials,
        entryPoints, distances$ 
        \State $\tilde{A} \gets A / \lambda$ \Comment{Convert
        to voxel grid coordinates}
        \State $currVoxelIdx \gets \lfloor \tilde{A} \rfloor$
        % Check if initial position is outside the grid
        \State $exitGrid \gets \text{false}$
        \If{any($\tilde{A} < 0$) or any($\tilde{A} \geq \text{shape}(materialGrid)$)}
            \State $exitGrid \gets \text{true}$
            \State \Return $C = A,\ crossedVoxels,\ crossedMaterials,\ entryPoints,\ distances,$ 
            \Statex \hspace*{\algorithmicindent + \widthof{\textbf{return} }}$exitGrid$
        \EndIf
        
        \State $currMaterial \gets materialGrid[\ currVoxelIdx\ ]$

        \While{$currMaterial$ is not air}
            \State Apply \autoref{alg:rayTraversalStep} to get $maxDist$ and $\tilde{A}$
            \State $distance \gets maxDist \cdot \lambda$ \Comment{Convert distance to world units in cm}
            \State Update arrays $\langle crossedVoxels, crossedMaterials, entryPoints, distances \rangle$ with 
            \Statex \hspace*{\algorithmicindent} $(currVoxelIdx, currMaterial, A, distance)$
            \State $currVoxelIdx \gets\lfloor \tilde{A} \rfloor$
            \State $A \gets A + \vec{\omega} \cdot distance$ \Comment{Update position in world coordinates}
            \If{any($\tilde{A} < 0$) or any($\tilde{A} \geq \text{shape}(materialGrid)$)}
                \State $exitGrid \gets \text{true}$
                \State \Return $C = A,\ crossedVoxels,\ crossedMaterials,\ entryPoints,\ distances,$ 
                \Statex \hspace*{\algorithmicindent + \algorithmicindent + \widthof{\textbf{return} }}$exitGrid$
            \EndIf
            \State $currMaterial \gets materialGrid[\ currVoxelIdx\ ]$
        \EndWhile
        \State \Return $C = A,\ crossedVoxels,\ crossedMaterials,\ entryPoints,\ distances,$ 
        \Statex \hspace*{\widthof{\textbf{return} }}$exitGrid$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\subsection{Compton Scattering}

The Compton scattering algorithm strictly follows the description in
Section~\ref{sec:physicsComptonScattering}. The algorithm implements the
Klein--Nishina formula (Eq.~\ref{eq:kleinNishina}) to sample the scattering
angle $\theta$ with the acceptance-rejection method. The energy update is
applied with the Compton formula (Eq.~\ref{eq:compton_formula}). Finally, the
new direction $\vec{\omega}'$ is computed with the sampled angles $\theta$ and
$\phi$.

The algorithm requires the following inputs:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The initial photon energy $E \in \mathbb{R}$.
    \item The initial (unit) direction of the photon $\vec{\omega} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
    \item Two random variables $u_1, u_2 \in [0,1]$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Compton Scattering (Klein--Nishina + Acceptance--Rejection)}
\label{alg:comptonScattering}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Initial photon energy $E$, unit direction $\vec{\omega}$
\Require Random inputs $u_1,u_2 \in [0,1]$ (reused until acceptance)
\Ensure Scattered energy $E'$ and unit direction $\vec{\omega}'$

\State $E_{\mathrm{rest}} \gets 0.511\,\mathrm{MeV}$ \Comment{Initialize electron rest energy}
\State $k \gets E/E_{\mathrm{rest}}$
\State $\zeta(k) \gets \dfrac{2(2k^2+2k+1)}{(2k+1)^3}$
\State $b(k) \gets \dfrac{1+\zeta/2}{1-\zeta/2}$,\quad $a(k) \gets 2\,(b-1)$
\State Define $f(k,x) \gets \dfrac{1}{(1+k(1-x))^2}\!\left(1+x^2+\dfrac{k^2(1-x)^2}{1+k(1-x)}\right)$
\State Define $h(k,x) \gets \dfrac{a}{b-x}$ \Comment{Envelope on $x\in[-1,1]$}

\Repeat \Comment{Acceptance--Rejection Sampling loop}
    \State Draw $r \sim \mathcal{U}(0,1)$
    \State $x \gets b - (b+1)\left(\dfrac{\zeta}{2}\right)^{r}$
    \State $\mathrm{acc} \gets \dfrac{f(k,x)}{h(k,x)}$
\Until{$u_1 \le \mathrm{acc}$}
\State $\theta \gets \arccos(x)$

\State $E' \gets \dfrac{E}{1+k(1-\cos\theta)}$ \Comment{Energy update (Compton formula \eqref{eq:compton_energy})}

\State $\phi \gets 2\pi u_2$ \Comment{Azimuthal angle}
\If{$|\omega_z|<0.999$} \State $\vec{v}\gets(0,0,1)$ \Else \State $\vec{v}\gets(1,0,0)$ \EndIf
\State $\vec{e}_1 \gets \dfrac{\vec{v}\times\vec{\omega}}{\|\vec{v}\times\vec{\omega}\|}$,\quad
       $\vec{e}_2 \gets \vec{\omega}\times\vec{e}_1$ \Comment{Orthonormal basis}

\State $\vec{\omega}' \gets \cos\theta\,\vec{\omega} + \sin\theta\bigl(\cos\phi\,\vec{e}_1+\sin\phi\,\vec{e}_2\bigr)$

\State \Return $E',\ \vec{\omega}'$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\subsection{Forced Fixed Detection}
\label{sc:forcedDetection}

The forced fixed detection algorithm is applied at each forced scatter point.
Here, the photon intensity distributes into multiple paths: 
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Primary intensity:} The part of the photon intensity that is directly forwarded to the boundary of the grid without further scattering and eventually contributes to the detector signal. $$ W \cdot \int \mu(x,E) $$
    \item \textbf{Absorption:} The part of the photon intensity that undergoes a photoelectric absorption at the forced scatter point and does not contribute to the detector signal. $$W \cdot \big(1 - \int \mu(x,E) \big) \cdot \frac{\mu_\textrm{PE}}{\mu_\textrm{PE}+\mu_\textrm{CS}}$$
    \item \textbf{Compton scattering:} The part of the photon intensity that undergoes Compton scattering at the forced scatter point and continues its path with updated energy and direction. $$W \cdot \big(1 - \int \mu(x,E) \big) \cdot \frac{\mu_\textrm{CS}}{\mu_\textrm{PE}+\mu_\textrm{CS}}$$
\end{itemize}

The implementation of the algorithm requires the following inputs:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The voxel grid $materialGrid \in \mathbb{Z}^{X \times Y \times Z}$
    representing the materials of the scene including the phantom. $X,Y,Z$ represent
    the shape of the grid.
    \item The voxel size $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^+$.
    \item The initial position $A_0 \in \mathbb{R}^3$, (unit) direction $\vec{\omega}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and energy $E_0 \in \mathbb{R}$ of the photon
\end{itemize}

For explanatory purposes, the algorithm is split into two parts: the step
algorithm (Algorithm~\ref{alg:forcedDetectionStep}) where one forced scatter
event is processed and the main algorithm
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:forcedDetectionMain}) where the step algorithm is applied
iteratively for $N$ forced scatter events.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
    \caption{Forced Fixed Detection: Step}
    \label{alg:forcedDetectionStep}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require Material grid $materialGrid \in \mathbb{Z}^{X \times Y \times
        Z}$, voxel size $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^+$
        \Require Initial position $A \in \mathbb{R}^3$, unit direction $\vec{\omega} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, energy $E$ and intensity $W$ of 
        \Statex \hspace*{\widthof{\textbf{qure: }}}the photon
        \Require Random values $u_1, u_2, u_3 \in [0,1]$
        \Require Attenuation functions $\mu(x,E), \mu_\textrm{CS}(x,E)$, $\mu_\textrm{PE}(x,E)$ for all materials

        \Ensure New position $A^\prime$, direction $\vec{\omega}^\prime$, energy $E^\prime$ and intensity $W^\prime$ of the scattered photon
        \Ensure Escaped position $\hat{C}$ and intensity $escapeIntensity$ of the escaped proportion

        \State Apply \autoref{alg:rayTraversalMain} to traverse the ray through
        the phantom and obtain exit position $C$ and arrays $materials,\
        distances$
        \State Initialize empty array $atts$
        \State $totalAtt \gets 0$
        
        \For{$j \gets 0$ to $len(materials)-1$}
            \State $material \gets materials[j]$
            \State $distance \gets distances[j]$
            \State $voxelAtt \gets \mu(\cdot, E)\cdot distances[j]$ \Comment{$\mu(\cdot,E)$ according to material in voxel}
            \State $atts[j] \gets voxelAtt$
            \State $totalAtt \gets totalAtt + voxelAtt$
        \EndFor
        \State $escapeProb \gets \exp(-totalAtt)$
        \State $attGoal \gets -\ln(1 - u_1 \cdot (1 - escapeProb))$

        \State iteratively find $J$ such that $\sum\limits_{j=0}^{J} atts[j] \leq attGoal < \sum\limits_{j=0}^{J+1} atts[j]$ \label{alg:forcedDetectionStep:line:freePathLength}

        \State $goalDist \gets \sum\limits_{j=0}^{J} distances[j] + \frac{attGoal - \sum\limits_{j=0}^{J} atts[j]}{\mu(\cdot, E)}$ \Comment{distance to next scatter point}
        \Statex \Comment{$\mu(\cdot,E)$ according to material in voxel $J+1$} \label{alg:forcedDetectionStep:line:freePathLengthEnd}
        \State $A^\prime \gets A + goalDist \cdot \vec{\omega}$
        \State $escapeIntensity \gets W \cdot escapeProb \cdot E$
        \State $W^\prime \gets W \cdot (1 - escapeProb) \cdot \frac{\mu_\textrm{CS}}{\mu_\textrm{CS} + \mu_\textrm{PE}}$
        \State Apply Algorithm~\textcolor{red}{TODO:REFERENCE-ALGORITHM} for Compton Scattering with direction $\vec{\omega}$, energy $E$ and random variables $u_2, u_3$ to obtain new direction $\vec{\omega}^\prime$ and energy $E^\prime$ \label{alg:forcedDetectionStep:line:compton}
        \State Apply \autoref{alg:rayTraversalMain} to traverse the ray from exit point $C$ of the phantom through air until it exits the grid and obtain exit position $\hat{C}$ \label{alg:forcedDetectionStep:line:ExitPoint}
        \State \Return $A^\prime, W^\prime, E^\prime, \vec{\omega}^\prime$ for the scattered photon, $escapeIntensity, \hat{C}$ for the escaped
        \Statex \hspace*{\widthof{\textbf{return} }} photon
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The algorithm applies the Beer-Lambert law (Eq.~\ref{eq:lambert_beer_law}) as
described in Section~\ref{sec:algorithmOverview} to compute the escape
probability of the photon. Further, the probability of Compton scattering is
implemented with an update on the intensity of the scattered photon. This yields
a physically accurate simulation by proportionally considering the attenuation
effects and the ratio of Compton scattering and photoelectric absorption.

For sampling the free path length until the next forced scatter point, equation
\eqref{eq:freePathLength} is applied. The integral $$\int_0^t \mu(A_i + s
\vec{\omega}_i, E_i) ds$$ is given by the cumulative sum of the attenuation
coefficients $\mu(\cdot, E_i)$ along the ray multiplied by the corresponding
distances within each voxel. Here, the algorithm iteratively finds the index $J$
such that the cumulative sum of the attenuation coefficients up to index $J$ is
less than or equal to the target attenuation $attGoal$ as described in line
\ref{alg:forcedDetectionStep:line:freePathLength} of the algorithm.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
    \caption{Forced Fixed Detection: Main Algorithm}
    \label{alg:forcedDetectionMain}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require Material grid $materialGrid \in \mathbb{Z}^{X \times Y \times
        Z}$, voxel size $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^+$
        \Require Initial position $A_0 \in \mathbb{R}^3$, unit direction $\vec{\omega}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^3$, energy $E_0$ of the photon
        \Require Number of forced scatter events $N$
        \Require $3N$ Random values $u_0,\ldots u_{3N-1} \in [0,1]$
        \Require Attenuation functions $\mu(x,E), \mu_\textrm{CS}(x,E)$, $\mu_\textrm{PE}(x,E)$ for all materials

        \Ensure Arrays $exitPoss^*, exitInts^*$ for primary intensities
        \Ensure Arrays $exitPoss, exitInts$ for scattered intensities

        \State Initialize empty arrays $exitPoss^*, exitInts^*$ \Comment{for primary intensities}
        \State Initialize empty arrays $exitPoss, exitInts$
        \State $W_0 \gets 1$ \Comment{Initial intensity of the photon}

        \For{$i \gets 0$ to $N-1$}
            \State Apply \autoref{alg:forcedDetectionStep} with inputs $A_i, \vec{\omega}_i, E_i, W_i, u_{3i}, u_{3i+1}, u_{3i+2}$ to obtain
            \Statex \hspace*{\algorithmicindent} $A_{i+1}, W_{i+1}, E_{i+1}, \vec{\omega}_{i+1}$ for the scattered photon and
            \Statex \hspace*{\algorithmicindent} $W^{\text{escape}}_{i+1}, C_i$ for the escaped photon
            \If{$i = 0$}
                \State Append $C_i, W^{\text{escape}}_{i+1}$ to $exitPoss^*, exitInts^*$
            \Else
                \State Append $C_i, W^{\text{escape}}_{i+1}$ to $exitPoss, exitInts$
            \EndIf
        \EndFor

        \State Apply \autoref{alg:rayTraversalMain} to traverse the photon from $A_N$ with direction $\vec{\omega}_N$ through the phantom and obtain exit position $C_N$ and arrays: $materials,\ distances$

        \State Initialize array $atts$ with attenuation coefficient $\mu(\cdot,
        E_N)$ according to material in $materials$

        \State $lastInt \gets W_N \cdot E_N \cdot \exp(- \langle distances , atts \rangle )$

        \State Append $C_N, lastInt$ to $exitPoss, exitInts$

        \State \Return $exitPoss^*, exitInts^*$ for primary intensities, $exitPoss, exitInts$ for scattered intensities
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The forced fixed detection algorithm then yields the exit positions and
according intensities for both the primary and scattered photons.

\section{Algorithm Composition}
\label{sec:algorithmComposition}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

For the implementation of the Photon Simulation Algorithm, the sub-algorithms from Section~\ref{sec:subAlgorithms} are composed.

The algorithm is initialized with the following parameters:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The voxel grid $materialGrid \in \mathbb{Z}^{X \times Y \times Z}$
    representing the materials of the scene including the phantom. $X,Y,Z$ represent
    the shape of the grid.
    \item The voxel size $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^+$.
    \item The sampling strategy: Monte Carlo / Halton / Sobol'.
    \item The source model: Point source / Extended source.
    \item The number of forced scatter events $N$.
\end{itemize}

The algorithm begins with initializing the random values according to the chosen sampling strategy. Then, the photon is initialized according to the source model.

As described in Section~\ref{sec:photonDirectionSampling}, the photon
initialization is applied by sampling the photon energy with
Algorithm~\ref{alg:photonEnergySampling} and evaluating the resulting function
with the first random variable $u_0$. Then, depending on the source model either
Algorithm~\ref{alg:pointSourceSampling} or
Algorithm~\ref{alg:extendedSourceSampling} is applied to sample the initial
direction or position of the photon respectively and set the other parameter
accordingly. Here, the random variables $u_1$ and $u_2$ are applied.

Then the photon is traversed through air to the phantom with Algorithm~\ref{alg:rayTraversalMain} to obtain the initial position $A_0$ in the phantom. Finally, the forced fixed detection algorithm (Algorithm~\ref{alg:forcedDetectionMain}) is applied with $N$ scatter events, the photon parameters and the remaining random values to obtain the arrays for primary intensities $exitPoss^*, exitInts^*$ and for scattered intensities $exitPoss, exitInts$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Photon Simulation Algorithm}
\label{alg:mainSimulation}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Material grid $materialGrid \in \mathbb{Z}^{X \times Y \times Z}$, voxel size $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^+$
\Require Sampling strategy: Monte Carlo / Halton / Sobol'
\Require Number of scatter events $N$

\State Initialize randoms $(u_i)_{i=0}^{3N+2} \in [0,1]^{3(N+1)}$ according to the sampling strategy

\State Initialize photon with position $S$, energy $E_0$, direction $\vec{\omega}_0$  according to the source model and the corresponding \autoref{alg:photonEnergySampling}-\ref{alg:extendedSourceSampling} applying 3 random values $u_0,u_1,u_2$ as described in Section~\ref{sec:photonDirectionSampling}

\State Traverse photon through air to phantom with \autoref{alg:rayTraversalMain} to obtain initial position $A_0$ in phantom

\State Apply \autoref{alg:forcedDetectionMain} given $N$ scatter events, the photon parameters and random values $(u_i)_{i=3}^{3N+2}$ to obtain the arrays $exitPoss^*, exitInts^*$ for primary intensities and $exitPoss, exitInts$ for scattered intensities

\State \Return $exitPoss^*, exitInts^*$ for primary intensities, $exitPoss, exitInts$ for scattered intensities
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm \ref{alg:mainSimulation} simulates the scattering of a single photon.
In practice, the algorithm is applied for a large number of photons to obtain
statistically significant results that are relevant for real world applications
such as CT imaging. In the implementation for this thesis, the algorithm is
further vectorized to simulate the scattering of multiple photons in parallel
and speed up the computation.
